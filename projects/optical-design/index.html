An adaptive optics system for the Euler Telescope

Turbulence in the atmosphere constantly distorts the light from a star, so a telescope sees a blurred, shimmering image. Adaptive optics (AO) corrects this as it happens: a sensor measures the distortion, and a deformable mirror changes shape to cancel it.

My task was to design a simple AO system behind the Euler Telescope: choose the lenses and where to place them, so that each part of the system receives the light in the form it needs.

Each lens has one job:

  • L1 shrinks the wide beam from the telescope to fit the small deformable mirror, and forms an image of the telescope's main mirror on it. That way, each point of the deformable mirror corrects one point of the main mirror.
  • L2 focuses the corrected light onto the science camera, so that the image of a star spans only a few pixels.
  • L3 and the pyramid feed the wavefront sensor. The pyramid splits the light into four images of the telescope mirror, and the differences between them show how the light is distorted. L3 sets the size of these images, and the pyramid's angle sets how far apart they land.

An atmospheric dispersion compensator

Air bends light slightly, and it bends blue light more than red. For a star low in the sky, this stretches its image into a tiny rainbow. An atmospheric dispersion compensator (ADC) is a set of prisms that spreads the colours by the same amount in the opposite direction, so they land back together.

I designed an ADC from two prisms made of different glasses. I worked out on paper how a ray bends at each surface, turned that into a Python ray trace, and used an optimiser to find the prism angles that best cancel the spread from the atmosphere.
